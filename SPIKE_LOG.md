## Measured latency on Sepolia (what a round will feel like)

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Transaction confirmation | 11–12 s (one block) |
| Encrypted input, browser with isolation headers | 6.5–7 s warm, 11 s first time |
| Encrypted input, browser without isolation / Node | 16–24 s |
| Decryption permit (once per session) | 1.8 s |
| User decrypt, 1 value | 3–4 s |
| User decrypt, 5 values in one call | 6 s |
| Public decrypt | 3–4 s |
| Ciphertext ready for decrypt after its tx | immediately (first decrypt after a tx took 5.6 s) |

A turn that needs an encrypted input costs roughly 7 s + 12 s. A turn whose secrecy comes from on-chain randomness costs 12 s,
plus 3–6 s to see your own hidden result.

## Proven capabilities (✅)
- On-chain encrypted randomness in arbitrary ranges, visible only to one player (S2).
- Encrypted player inputs (S3).
- Blind computation over hidden state that publishes exactly one bit (S4) and verifies it on-chain with replay protection (S5).
- Five distinct cards dealt from 52 in one transaction with no trusted dealer (S6).
- Official cUSDT mock: mint, wrap, stake into a game contract with a public or hidden amount, pay the winner (S8, S9).
- Zama SDK in the browser: encrypt, permit, batched private decrypt, public decrypt (S12, S13).
- A complete three-card poker hand — deal, blind ranking, comparison, and a hidden-hand opponent that can bluff — in two transactions (S15).

## Works with caveats (⚠️)
- ~25 chained encrypted steps per transaction — mitigation: tree-shaped reductions, split work across turns (S7).
- A short balance stakes 0 without reverting — mitigation: account with the returned handle; reveal a "funded" bit before seating (S10).
- Encrypted inputs take 6.5–24 s — mitigation: isolation headers on the host; designs where the chain supplies the secret (S11, S12).
- Stale reads on the public RPC — mitigation: block-pinned reads with retry (S14).

## Dead ends (❌)
- One-transaction wrap via ERC-1363 on the Sepolia mock USDT (S1).
- Full 52-card encrypted shuffle in one transaction — ruled out by S6 cost growth and the S7 depth limit; deal small hands instead.

## Surprises worth exploiting
- Batched user decryption is nearly free: five hidden values in 6 s. A whole hand can appear at once.
- One permit signature covers the session, so after the first prompt, looking at your own hidden state needs no wallet pop-up.
- The mock USDT is mintable by anyone, so the app can onboard a judge with test chips without a faucet hunt.
- Public decryption is fast (3–4 s) and anyone can submit the proof, so the losing player cannot stall a reveal.

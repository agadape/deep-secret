# Cross-ecosystem census of 2026 web3 hackathons with announced winners (platforms and multi-chain organizers)

Research date: 2026-11-16. Scope: hackathons held in calendar 2026 with winners announced by that date; catch-all census plus deep dive on organizers that do not belong to one chain.

Method note (read first): the shared web-search budget for this research session ran out part-way through this task. Everything after that point was done by opening known organizer pages directly. Several planned sweeps were therefore never run (regional mega-events in India / Japan / Africa, university events, Farcaster, World Build 3, ETHPrague, EasyA Hong Kong). These are listed as gaps, not as "nothing found". DoraHacks pages returned HTTP 405 to the fetch tool, and circle.com, coinbase.com, kucoin.com and mexc.com failed with a local TLS certificate error, so facts from those sites are at search-snippet level only and are marked "(snippet)". The SKALE recap page returned HTTP 429 on four attempts, so its contents are also "(snippet)".

Legend used in tables: "n/c" = not confirmed. "(snippet)" = taken from a search-result summary of the cited page, page itself not opened.

## 1. Master calendar: every major 2026 web3 hackathon with announced winners

### Takeaway
I confirmed about 30 major 2026 hackathons with announced winners and a further ~20 that ran in 2026 but whose winners I could not confirm. Dedicated gaming tracks were rare at flagship events in 2026: Sui Overflow, ETHDenver, Chainlink Convergence, Monad Metropolis, 0G APAC and Colosseum Frontier all ran without one, and game-like projects won inside "consumer", "prediction market" or "agent" tracks instead.

## 2. Deep dive: multi-chain and cross-ecosystem organizers with 2026 winners

### Takeaway
Among cross-ecosystem organizers, only two produced clear game winners that I could confirm: the SKALE / Coinbase / Google x402 hackathon (a game took first overall) and the YGG Play × Verse8 "Casual Degen" track at BuidlHack. Chainlink Convergence had no gaming track and no VRF game among its winners, but it produced the most relevant hidden-information designs (sealed-bid auctions and private prediction markets).

### Cited Findings

**Chainlink — Convergence (Feb 6–Mar 8, 2026)**
- 554 submissions, described as record-breaking; tracks were DeFi & Tokenization, CRE & AI, Prediction Markets, Risk & Compliance, Privacy, Autonomous Agents, plus World and Tenderly sponsor tracks — [winners blog](https://chain.link/blog/convergence-hackathon-winners), [winners page](https://chain.link/hackathon/winners).
- First places: FlowVault (DeFi), SentinelCRE (CRE & AI), TAPL (Prediction Markets), Aegis-Gate (Risk & Compliance), SSL Stealth Settlement Layer (Privacy), InControl.finance (Autonomous Agents), Cervile Finance (World Mini App), Creble (World ID), MaskBid (Tenderly) — [winners page](https://chain.link/hackathon/winners).
- The winners blog does not mention VRF anywhere, and no winner is described as a VRF-based game — [winners blog](https://chain.link/blog/convergence-hackathon-winners).
- No judge quotes were published; the blog only notes a shift toward "full-stack systems" combining off-chain and on-chain parts — [winners blog](https://chain.link/blog/convergence-hackathon-winners).
- Entry requirements published: a CRE workflow as the orchestration layer, an external API or data source integrated with a chain, a successful simulation or live deployment, a 3–5 minute public video, a public repo, and a README linking every file that uses Chainlink — [prizes page](https://chain.link/hackathon/prizes).

### Inferences

That is a commit-then-enclave-reveal design. None computed on encrypted state on-chain, and none kept information hidden after resolution. An FHEVM game that compares or updates encrypted values during play, without a reveal step or a trusted decryptor, would be doing something none of these winners did.
- The problems those winners named (sniping, front-running, position leakage, Sybil bidding) are the standard justification for sealed bids. A Zama entry can borrow that framing for a bidding or bluffing game.
- Bank or Plank is a push-your-luck game: a public, simple risk decision repeated quickly. Its hazard rolls are a natural candidate for encrypted randomness, though that is my extrapolation, not something the game does.
- Simon Stakes shows the lightest viable chain integration that still placed: off-chain play plus a signed score attestation. It placed third in a sponsor track, not first.

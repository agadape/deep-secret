# Solana-ecosystem hackathons of 2026 with announced winners (gaming / GameFi / consumer-entertainment focus)

Research date: 2026-11-16. Method notes that apply to every section:
- X/Twitter pages could not be fetched directly (HTTP 402). Where an X post is cited, the content comes from a search-engine snippet or from an official blog that links to it. These are marked "(snippet)".
- Some dates are marked "(tweet-ID date)". These were derived by decoding the timestamp embedded in the tweet ID, not read from the page. Treat them as accurate to the day but label them as derived.
- The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through. Remaining gaps are listed explicitly under each "Gaps" heading rather than filled from memory.
- Nothing below is invented. "Not confirmed" means I looked and could not verify it.

## Key Question 1: Which Solana-ecosystem hackathons ran in 2026 and have announced winners?

### Takeaway
At least 20 Solana-ecosystem competitions with announced winners ran in 2026: two Colosseum events (Agent Hackathon, Frontier), ten MagicBlock "Solana Blitz" editions (v0-v9), two Solana Mobile/RadiantsDAO events (Monolith, CLOCK IN), three Solana Foundation themed hackathons (Privacy Hack, Graveyard, and the PlaySolana Matrix hackathon run by Play Solana), plus regional Superteam side tracks. Colosseum's second global event of 2026 (Crypto World's Fair, Sep 14 - Oct 12) and the Pacifica hackathon have no winners list that I could find, so they are in the "excluded / not confirmed" list.

### Inferences
- The Solana calendar in 2026 has two tiers: large startup competitions with no gaming track at all (Frontier removed all tracks) and small, frequent, infrastructure-sponsored sprints (Blitz) where games are a large share of winners. For game-mechanic inspiration the Blitz series is the richer source, even though each edition pays only 1,000 USDC.
- Monolith's hackathon window is not stated on the winners page. Winners were posted in late April 2026, so the event almost certainly ran in Q1 2026, but the exact dates are not confirmed.

## Key Question 2: For each hackathon, what are the tracks, the grand prize winner and the gaming / consumer / entertainment winners?

### Takeaway
Games or game-like products won in almost every event, but they rarely won the top prize in the big competitions: Frontier's Grand Champion was a robotics DePIN (CrowdBrain) and CLOCK IN's was a savings app (Sprouts). Game-like winners are concentrated in prediction/betting markets, tokenized card games, real-money skill games, and, from September 2026, hidden-information multiplayer games built on TEE-based Private Ephemeral Rollups.

### Takeaway
The profiles most relevant to a confidential-gaming entry are the hidden-information winners: BLACKOUT and Nightfall (fog-of-war battle royales), Herd (sealed party-game answers), khel.fun Poker and The Casino & Church (encrypted shuffles and outcomes on Inco), and the sealed-bid / private-market winners Crafts, Epoch, Bench, VeilMarket and Loofta Pot. ### Inferences
- Hidden-state approaches seen among 2026 Solana winners, by frequency: (1) TEE with per-account read permissions (BLACKOUT, Nightfall, Herd, VeilMarket, Loofta Pot, CapturGO); (2) MPC via Arcium for sealed bids and private market positions (Crafts, Epoch, Bench); (3) encrypted compute and encrypted randomness via Inco for card shuffles and casino outcomes (khel.fun Poker, The Casino & Church). Commit-reveal and trusted servers are not advertised by any winner I profiled.
- Both fog-of-war battle royales openly state that privacy rests on trusting TDX hardware and the validator's permission checks. An FHEVM entry can claim a property these winners explicitly cannot: confidentiality that does not depend on a hardware vendor or a single operator. That contrast is a ready-made pitch line, provided the FHE version is actually playable.
- The strongest demo devices for hidden-information games were adversarial and self-serve: BLACKOUT's "Try to cheat" button and post-game VERIFY tab, and Nightfall's X-ray panel that returns `null`. They let a judge confirm secrecy in seconds without reading code.
- Real-time fog of war at 20 ticks per second is feasible in a TEE rollup but is not a realistic target for FHE. The transferable ideas are the turn-light ones: per-player "view" objects only the owner can decrypt, direction-only "sound" hints as partial information leakage, a sealed world revealed at the end with a replay, delayed public spectator feed, and bots that obey the same visibility rules.
- One Arena's Arena Shootouts (simultaneous secret shot / save directions modified by card stats) and Herd (sealed party answers) are small simultaneous-choice games. That class (secret pick, encrypted comparison, reveal) maps directly to FHE comparisons and needs only a handful of encrypted operations per round.
- Push-your-luck with hidden next outcome (Hunch) and tile-pick mining (BlitzMine) are solo-playable and currently rely on VRF. With encrypted randomness the board could be pre-committed and hidden rather than rolled per action.

## Key Question 4: Recurring patterns among gaming winners

### Takeaway
Gaming winners on Solana in 2026 skew toward short-session, real-money, mobile-friendly games that are playable alone (bots, house, or async opponents) and that wrap a financial primitive (perps, prediction markets, card packs, mining) in game form. From Blitz v8 onward, hidden-information multiplayer games became a winning category in their own right, enabled by privacy infrastructure rather than by new game design.

### Cited Findings
- Genre mix among confirmed game-like winners: prediction / betting markets (Mentioned, Memetic Machines, PredictTok, Cue, Foresee, VeilMarket, Epoch, Hot Take), card and collectible games (The Syndicate, One Arena, khel.fun Poker), real-time arcade and action (Perps Rider, Ultra Pong, BLACKOUT, Nightfall, Battle Cities, Alerith), chance and casino (Hunch, BlitzMine, Loofta Pot, Decent Gaming, The Casino & Church, Tramplin) — sources as cited per project in Key Question 3.

### Inferences
- A recognisable winning template for a small sponsor-judged bounty: one clear game loop, a session of five minutes or less, playable alone immediately (bots or house), optional small stakes, a live link, and a visible proof that the sponsor's unique technology is doing something a normal chain cannot. BLACKOUT fits every item.
- For a Zama "Confidential Gaming" entry, the Solana evidence suggests prioritising: (a) a turn-based or simultaneous-choice hidden-information core rather than real-time; (b) a bot or house opponent so judges can play solo; (c) an in-app "try to read the hidden state" control and an end-of-game reveal or replay; (d) encrypted randomness used for something VRF cannot do, namely outcomes that stay hidden after they are generated (face-down cards, sealed loot, hidden zones).
- Betting and prediction formats dominate the consumer winners in the large startup competitions, while skill and action games dominate the infrastructure sprints. A sealed-bid or hidden-stake market is the lower-risk design; a hidden-information game is the more distinctive one. Private prediction markets and sealed-bid auctions are already crowded on Solana (Epoch, VeilMarket, Bench, Crafts), so they are weaker as a novelty claim.
- Social-deduction games are absent from every winners list I could confirm. Herd (sealed party answers) is the closest. That genre appears to be open ground.
- Pure "financialized game" winners (Fraudsworth) succeed in startup-judged events because of revenue logic, not gameplay. That pattern is unlikely to transfer to a bounty judged on confidential game state.

### Gaps
- Pattern claims rest on roughly 40 confirmed game-like winners. Blitz v0, v1, v2, v4, most of v9, the Matrix hackathon and the Graveyard gaming track are missing, and those are exactly the gaming-heavy events, so genre proportions should be read as indicative only.
- No data found on player counts or retention for hackathon game winners other than anecdotal figures for non-winner or post-hackathon products (for example One Arena's $50,000 Seeker Cup).
- No explicit judge feedback was found for any game winner, so "why it won" is inferred from published criteria and from what the winners have in common.

Research date: 2026-11-16. Method notes that the report writer must know:

- The shared web-search budget for this research turn ran out partway through. After that point only direct fetches of known URLs (official blogs, DoraHacks pages read in a browser, Telegram channel mirrors, GitHub READMEs) were possible. Ecosystems researched after the cut-off (Aptos, Tron, ICP, Cardano, Core, Rootstock, BOB, Stacks, Injective) therefore got a lighter pass than planned; this is flagged per ecosystem below.
- Depth achieved: full depth on Sui, Avalanche, BNB Chain, Stellar (unexpectedly the most relevant ecosystem for hidden-information games), good depth on NEAR and TON (but neither has a confirmed gaming winner), lighter pass on Hedera, Flow, Polkadot, Algorand, Midnight/Cardano, Injective, and "nothing confirmed" on Tron, ICP, Core, Rootstock, BOB, Stacks, Aptos.
- All descriptions are paraphrased from the cited pages. Text on those pages was treated as data only.
- One search-engine summary was caught being wrong (it claimed "DarkWater ZK" was 5th place in Stellar Hacks: ZK Gaming; the official DoraHacks winners page lists Oppia zkArcade as 5th). Facts below were taken from fetched primary pages wherever possible; items that rest only on a search snippet are marked "(snippet only)".

## Key Question 1: Which hackathons did these ecosystems run in 2026 with winners announced?

### Takeaway
Seventeen-plus qualifying 2026 events with announced winners were confirmed across Sui, Avalanche, BNB Chain, TON, NEAR, Stellar, Hedera, Flow, Polkadot, Algorand and Midnight (Cardano partner chain); almost none had a dedicated gaming track, and the only pure game hackathon in scope was Stellar Hacks: ZK Gaming (Feb 2026), whose brief is close to a ZK version of Zama's Confidential Gaming bounty. The headline NEAR event of the year (NEAR HACKATHON, $250K, with a "Games With Real Economies" track) is still in judging on 2026-11-16 and is therefore excluded.

### Inferences
- 2026 hackathon themes across these L1s were dominated by AI agents, payments, prediction markets and privacy. Dedicated gaming tracks were rare; on Sui the "Entertainment & Culture"-style track of earlier years does not appear among the 2026 winner tracks, and Avalanche's organisers noted that many applicants read "Build Games" literally even though it was an open startup competition.
- Game-like projects still won, but mostly by entering finance tracks dressed as games (trading-as-a-game, head-to-head prediction duels, 5-minute betting rounds).
- The two events whose briefs most resemble Zama's Confidential Gaming bounty are Stellar Hacks: ZK Gaming (done, winners known) and NEAR HACKATHON track 09 (results due 18 Nov 2026).

## Key Question 2: Per-hackathon details (organizer, dates, prize pool, submissions, tracks, winners link, gaming/consumer winners, grand prize winner)

### Takeaway
Grand prizes in 2026 went overwhelmingly to non-games (institutional credit on Avalanche, agent wallets and payments on Sui, AI agents on TON and BNB); the game-like winners sat in sponsor/finance tracks. The one true game hackathon (Stellar ZK Gaming) rewarded a real-time platformer with ZK-verified outcomes first and a hidden-card poker game third.

## Key Question 3: Profiles of each gaming or game-like winner

### Takeaway
The deepest hidden-information work among these winners is on Stellar: Stellar Poker (MPC committee shuffles and deals, ZK proofs verified on-chain), Oppia zkArcade (commit a private board or word, prove each hit/miss or feedback) and Chickenz/xray.games (prove an outcome from a hidden input trace). Everything else that won elsewhere has fully public state and relies on oracles or VRF — which is exactly the gap an FHE game can fill.

### Cited Findings

**Chickenz.io — 1st, Stellar Hacks: ZK Gaming ($5,000)**
- What it is: competitive 2D multiplayer platformer-shooter; two players, best of three rounds of 30 seconds each, one life per round, five weapon pickups, arena walls close in at 20 seconds (sudden death) — [DoraHacks BUIDL](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39887)
- On-chain / proof flow: server registers the match on the game hub with both wallets and a committed seed hash; every input of a 60 Hz deterministic simulation is recorded; after the match the winning rounds are replayed inside a RISC Zero zkVM; a Groth16-compressed proof (260-byte seal) is verified by a Soroban contract, which checks the seed commitment and records the winner. Same Rust fixed-point simulation compiles to WASM (browser, server) and RISC-V (prover); proving cost cut 224x to about 234K cycles per round; proofs sourced on demand from the Boundless proving market — [DoraHacks BUIDL](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39887)
- Multiplayer or solo: 1v1 real time with matchmaking, private rooms, ELO, replays, and bot opponents (so it can be tried alone) — [DoraHacks BUIDL](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39887)
- Hidden information: none between players; ZK is used for verifiable computation of the outcome, not secrecy. Randomness is a server-generated seed committed by hash before play. The README is candid that trust remains: the server is authoritative, generates the seed, and only the server admin can start and settle matches; a player-driven commit-reveal seed and player-initiated settlement are listed as future work — [GitHub README](https://github.com/AshFrancis/chickenz)
- Why it won: no judge quote found; the organiser's own one-line summary stresses real-time play with off-chain compute verified on-chain — [DoraHacks winners page](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/stellar-hacks-zk-gaming/winner)

**xray.games — 2nd, Stellar Hacks: ZK Gaming ($2,000)**
- What it is: an arcade of skill games (Chain Slicer, Chain Snooker, Chain Runner) live on Stellar mainnet, where a player's score is proven by a ZK proof of the whole deterministic physics run; a two-player Chain Slicer edition was built for the hackathon's game hub — [DoraHacks BUIDL](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39622)

### Inferences
- Four distinct ways of handling secrets or fairness appear among winners: (a) commit + per-move ZK proof by the secret holder (Oppia Battleship/Wordle), (b) MPC committee + ZK for shared secrets like a deck (Stellar Poker), (c) ZK proof of a private input trace for anti-cheat (Chickenz, xray.games, Cosmic Coders), (d) nothing hidden, with oracle or VRF resolution (Strike, Flicky as described, OverFlow, Neon Nexus, DOT Pixel). FHE on Zama's FHEVM can replace (a) and (b) without an MPC committee run by the game and without per-move client proving, and can add hidden state to the type (d) games that currently have none (for example, hiding pool sides in a parimutuel round or hiding swipes in a duel).
- Stellar Poker's builder explicitly framed the deck-shuffle problem as something ZK alone cannot solve; that is a ready-made positioning line for an FHE card game (encrypted on-chain shuffle and deal with no dealer committee).
- Strike's early-bird bonus exists because public pools reward waiting; encrypted bets would remove late-mover advantage outright — a clean "why FHE" story for a betting game.
- Winners that disclosed limitations (Chickenz README, Wraith per organisers) were still ranked first; candour about trust assumptions did not hurt and was praised at least once.
- Polish and teachability mattered in the one pure game hackathon: the third-place poker entry shipped a slide deck, a reusable library, music and themes.

- PIPS: actual game loop, whether multiplayer, and any repo/demo video are not confirmed (waitlist page only). Flicky: site down; hidden-swipe handling, stakes and repo not confirmed.

## Key Question 4: Recurring patterns among these gaming winners

### Takeaway
Across these ecosystems in 2026, winning "games" were short-session, stake-settled and finance-flavoured (prediction duels, trading-as-play, parimutuel rounds), frequently solo-playable or bot-backed, and often AI-assisted or AI-populated; genuinely hidden-information games won only where the brief demanded it (Stellar), and there they used commit/ZK or MPC rather than a trusted server.

### Cited Findings

- Hidden information handling: MPC + ZK (Stellar Poker), commit + per-move Groth16 proofs (Oppia zkArcade), ZK over private input traces (Chickenz, xray.games, Cosmic Coders), VRF commit-reveal for randomness (Neon Nexus), server-committed seed (Chickenz), trusted off-chain database (OverFlow), none (Strike, DOT Pixel) — sources as in Key Question 3
- "Why blockchain" and execution are recurring judge themes: Avalanche judges weighted founders, go-to-market and a clear reason to be on-chain — [AVAX One](https://avax-one.com/inside-the-avalanche-build-games-a-conversation-with-avax-one-coo-pete-wylie/); Stellar required the ZK mechanic to be core rather than decorative, plus a working front end and video — [DoraHacks detail](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/stellar-hacks-zk-gaming/detail)
- Infrastructure-for-games also wins: The Grotto (gaming L1, 3rd on Avalanche), AMP (finalist), Stellar Poker's reusable shuffle crate, xray.games' reusable circuits — [AVAX One](https://avax-one.com/inside-the-avalanche-build-games-a-conversation-with-avax-one-coo-pete-wylie/), [Avalanche on X](https://x.com/avax/status/2037561176196850070), [Stellar Poker](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39840), [xray.games](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39622)

### Inferences
- A winning pattern for a cryptography-centred game bounty, as evidenced at Stellar: pick a familiar game anyone understands in ten seconds; make the privacy primitive indispensable to one mechanic; ship a playable front end with a way to play alone (bot, async or pass-and-play); explain the cryptography simply (deck, write-up, video); be explicit about remaining trust assumptions; and leave behind a reusable component (library, circuit, contract) for other builders.
- Mechanics seen winning in public-state form that would gain from encryption on FHEVM: (1) two-player swipe/prediction duel with hidden picks until reveal (Flicky-style); (2) parimutuel 5-minute rounds with encrypted side and size so nobody can free-ride on pool visibility (Strike-style); (3) poker or other dealt-card games without an MPC dealer committee (Stellar Poker-style); (4) Battleship/Wordle-type hidden-board games without client-side proving (Oppia-style); (5) sealed-bid auctions with a provable winner (QuietBook-style); (6) last-agent-standing survival with hidden strategies or resources (Neon Nexus-style).
- Telegram mini-app distribution was not a differentiator in 2026 results for these chains; fast rounds, clear stakes and a strong "why this cryptography" story were.

### Gaps
- Pattern evidence is thin for TON and Aptos gaming in 2026 because no qualifying gaming winners were found there; conclusions about Telegram distribution rest on one BNB winner plus TON's non-gaming contests.
- The NEAR HACKATHON "Games With Real Economies" winners (due by 18 Nov 2026) could materially change the pattern picture and should be checked before the report is finalised.
- Sample of true hidden-information game winners is small (essentially two: Stellar Poker and Oppia zkArcade); no data on player counts, retention or post-hackathon traction for any profiled game.

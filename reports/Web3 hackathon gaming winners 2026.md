# Small secrets win confidential game jams

About fifty crypto hackathons held in 2026 have winners this research could confirm, and the gaming picture inside them is lopsided. The flagship events (Colosseum Frontier, Monad Metropolis, Sui Overflow, Chainlink Convergence, every ETHGlobal) ran no gaming track and gave their top prizes to AI-agent, payments and infrastructure projects; real hidden-information games won only where the brief demanded hidden state: the Inco x Megapot Summer Game Jam (Mentalist, Zunno, Hextract), Stellar Hacks: ZK Gaming (Chickenz.io, xray.games, Stellar Poker), the MLH x Midnight July hack (Moonray, AmongUs Midnight) and MagicBlock's Solana Blitz weekends. In those comparables the podium favoured one small, legible secret with money attached over ports of card games: a staked murder mystery beat Uno and Hold'em at Inco, and poker placed third at Stellar. Almost every hidden-state winner leaned on a hardware enclave, an MPC committee or commit-reveal; only one placed game (AEGYLAX, an honorable mention) ran on FHE, and no confirmed 2026 poker winner documents ranking hands on encrypted cards or protecting folded hands. For the Zama Season 5 "Confidential Gaming" bounty this keeps candidate A (three-card poker against a bluffing hidden-hand dealer) in front on readiness and solo play but strips its novelty outside the Zama field, lifts candidate B (sealed-bid crate auction with private clues) to the most original direction, and leaves candidate C (code-breaker race) the weakest as stated because a Mastermind-style game already exists on Zama's FHEVM and guessing games are a sponsor template. Three events the user named are still open: NEAR's November hackathon (results due 18 November), Colosseum's Crypto World's Fair and Pacifica have no confirmed winners. The evidence base has real holes: search budget ran out for several researchers, X and DoraHacks pages were often unreadable, and almost no verbatim judge commentary exists, so every "why it won" below is organizer description or inference unless marked otherwise.

## Seven mechanics recur among winners, and four make a secret the game

Grouping the game winners by what the player does, not by chain, shows where hidden state was the game and where it was absent. Four groups build play on a secret (a sealed answer, hidden hands, sealed orders, a hidden layout), one hides bids and positions but sells itself as finance, one proves a private input trace as anti-cheat, and the largest hides nothing at all. The first table covers every winner or placer whose core loop depends on a secret. A reader should note the technology column: Inco Lightning and MagicBlock Private Ephemeral Rollups are enclave-based (Intel TDX). One winner's own README states that Inco is TEE-based compute and is neither FHE nor zero knowledge ([Mentalist](https://github.com/adipundir/mentalist)).

| **AmongUs Midnight** (MLH x Midnight, gaming winner) | Social deduction retrofitted onto an open-source Among Us clone | Roles committed per seat, votes spent by nullifier, role revealed on ejection; gameplay stays on a server | Multiplayer | Organizer: people wanted to play it | [code](https://github.com/SoumyaEXE/Among-Midnight) |
| **Oppia zkArcade** (Stellar ZK Gaming, 5th) | Battleship and a Wordle-style game | Board or word committed; holder proves each hit or feedback in the browser | 2 players | Not published | [demo](https://oppia-zkarcade.vercel.app), [code](https://github.com/Oppia-Software-Labs/zkArcade) |

### One sealed answer, many staked picks

Mentalist is the clearest template. Its entire cryptographic footprint is one encrypted integer and one encrypted equality per player, the long "thinking" phase costs no transaction, and the money layer is a pari-mutuel pot ([Mentalist README](https://github.com/adipundir/mentalist)). The builder states two reasons the design fails without encryption: an operator holding the answer could change it, and a hash commitment over three to eight suspects is brute-forced at once ([Mentalist README](https://github.com/adipundir/mentalist)). The same README lists the weaknesses a successor can attack: the seven cases are hand-written and single-use, the chain cannot check that the sealed answer is the right one, and security rests on hardware. AEGYLAX uses the same shape with a continuous target and purchasable noisy hints ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners)), and Moonray uses it with a sealed score instead of a sealed pick ([Moonray README](https://github.com/kaleababayneh/moonray)).

### Hidden hands

Card games with private hands are the most crowded hidden-information genre of the year: Zunno (2nd) and pi River (honorable mention) at Inco, khel.fun Poker at the Solana Privacy Hack, Stellar Poker (3rd) at Stellar, and Cipher21 at Zama ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners), [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/stellar-hacks-zk-gaming/winner), [Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/announcing-the-developer-program-mainnet-season-2-winners)). Sponsors treat poker as the default idea: Inco's jam brief lists poker and blackjack first and Inco ships a ready-made confidential deck contract ([Inco brief](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-resources-and-what-to-build), [Inco docs](https://docs.inco.org/games/confidential-deck)). Two technical facts stand out. Zunno does not evaluate rules on encrypted cards; the player submits the claimed card with an attestation and the contract checks it against the stored handle, then runs Uno rules in plain Solidity ([Zunno README](https://github.com/ayush4345/zunno-inco)). And pi River's README does not say whether its hand evaluator runs on encrypted values, nor how folded cards are treated ([pi River](https://github.com/thesithunyein/pi-river)). No source found describes fold handling, side pots or collusion mitigation for any encrypted poker project.

### Simultaneous sealed orders and fog of war

Hextract packs a player's two actions into a single ciphertext, and the second player's submission triggers resolution, so there is no separate resolve step ([Hextract README](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT)). Its scans return a coarse proximity band, not a position, and enemy decoys create false signals. The builder's argument for sealing is that on a public board the second mover, or any spectator, could always snipe ([Hextract README](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT)). On Solana the same idea appeared in real time: BLACKOUT and Nightfall are fog-of-war battle royales running at 20 ticks per second inside an enclave rollup, with bots filling seats, though both placings are self-reported by their builders ([BLACKOUT](https://github.com/Anshv784/blackout), [Nightfall](https://github.com/codeswithroh/nightfall)). One Arena, a Colosseum Frontier Top 25 team, runs a penalty-shootout mode in which one player secretly picks a shot direction and the other a save direction; how those choices are concealed is not documented ([Genfinity](https://genfinity.io/2026/10/15/rosentica-natsuki-one-arena-solana-rwa-collectibles-application-layer-interview/)).

### Hidden layout, push your luck

Glass Bridge Royale draws its whole map as one encrypted word and derives three disclosure modes from it: unopened panels are visible to nobody, a stepped-on panel to everybody, and a "bribed" future panel to one address ([Glass Bridge README](https://github.com/gks2022004/glass-bridge-royale)). Hunch (Blitz v6, 3rd) and BlitzMine (Blitz v7 winner) are solo push-your-luck games whose next outcome comes from a VRF roll instead of a pre-sealed board ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/magicblock-solana-blitz-v6-41-mobile-apps-three-winners-one-weekend), [MagicBlock](https://www.magicblock.xyz/blog/august2026-recap)). Bank or Plank, the first-place Casual Degen game, is the same decision with public state: bank your gold before a bad roll or the five-minute clock wipes it ([BlockchainGamer](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/news/42097/bank-or-plank-wins-verse8-ygg-plays-buidlhack-2026-hackathon/)).

### Sealed bids and hidden positions, sold as finance

The largest group of "game-like" hidden-state winners are not games. Crafts (sealed-bid uniform-price auction), Bench (private opportunity market) and Epoch (prediction market with hidden side and size) won Arcium's track using MPC ([Arcium](https://arcium.substack.com/p/winners-of-solana-privacy-hack)); Zerk and SealPad won at Zama with encrypted bets and sealed-bid token sales ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/announcing-the-developer-program-mainnet-season-2-winners)); NightPool won at Midnight ([Midnight](https://midnight.network/blog/celebrating-seven-winners-from-mlh-x-midnight-july-hack)); MaskBid, OneShot and VeritasX won Chainlink tracks by encrypting bids client-side and decrypting in an enclave after the deadline ([MaskBid](https://chain.link/hack-26/projects/maskbid-the-dark-auction), [OneShot](https://chain.link/hack-26/projects/oneshot)); QuietBook won a Stellar track ([Stellar](https://developers.stellar.org/meetings/2026/08/13)); and at ETHGlobal, Trade Royale, GhostBet, Daemon Hall, Voidmarket and CircEveryBid took sponsor prizes using a shielded pool, a private chain or plain commit-reveal ([Trade Royale](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/trade-royale-esviu), [Daemon Hall](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/daemon-hall-d14u4), [Voidmarket](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/voidmarket-hph0e)). Judges everywhere accept "sealed bids stop sniping" as a reason for privacy. No winner found presents bidding as a game.

### Proof-of-play skill games

Chickenz.io (1st at Stellar) is a 1v1 platformer-shooter in three 30-second rounds whose outcome is replayed in a zkVM and verified on-chain; nothing is hidden between players, and the README admits the server is still authoritative ([Chickenz BUIDL](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39887), [README](https://github.com/AshFrancis/chickenz)). xray.games (2nd) and Cosmic Coders (4th) likewise prove a score from a private input trace ([xray.games](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39622), [Cosmic Coders](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39826)). This is anti-cheat, a ZK strength with no natural FHE equivalent.

### Public-state stake games, agent arenas and worlds

The remaining winners hide nothing. Trading dressed as play recurs: Rip Trade (cards that are five-second leveraged trades) and Chart Arena won Monad's Consumer track ([Monad](https://monad.xyz/metropolis)); PIPS won Sui's DeepBook track as a handheld-console skin over trading ([Sui](https://www.sui.io/blog/sui-overflow-2026-winners)); TAPL won Chainlink's Prediction Markets track with a one-tap price grid ([TAPL](https://chain.link/hack-26/projects/tapl)); Perps Rider placed second at Blitz v6 ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/magicblock-solana-blitz-v6-41-mobile-apps-three-winners-one-weekend)); ETH Arcade, Défi, Blip Market and PulsePlay were ETHGlobal finalists ([ETH Arcade](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/eth-arcade-96wyn), [Défi](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/defi-e9zii)). Strike on BNB is a five-minute parimutuel round in Telegram that pays early bettors a bonus because public pools reward waiting ([Strike](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39607)). Agent arenas and worlds form a second cluster: NoJohns and The Reef at Moltiverse ([NoJohns](https://github.com/ScavieFae/nojohns), [The Reef](https://github.com/ACRLABSDEV/the-reef-public)), Clan World at ETHGlobal Open Agents ([Clan World](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/clan-world-aelder-g4i6y)), Neon Nexus on Flow ([Neon Nexus](https://github.com/goncalorap123/neon-nexus)). Fully on-chain rule engines without secrets include Void Tactics at ETHGlobal New York ([Void Tactics](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/void-tactics-ag25f)), and the EVE Frontier winners were management, bounty and intel systems inside a live MMO ([BlockchainGamer](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/news/42111/eve-frontier-x-sui-2026-hackathon-winners-revealed/)). The Syndicate, a mafia card league in Colosseum's Top 25, runs sessions under two minutes with asynchronous PvP; its fairness mechanism is not documented ([Colosseum](https://colosseum.com/arena/projects/explore/the-syndicate)).

## The closest comparables put deduction and completeness ahead of poker

Three 2026 events had a brief close to Zama's: build a game in which hidden state is essential. Their rubrics and placings are the best available proxy for how a "Confidential Gaming" panel thinks, with the caveat that none of them published judge commentary on individual games.

**Inco x Megapot Summer Game Jam.** The build ran from 29 July to 14 August with an Inco track paying $3,000, $1,500 and $500 in USDC ([Inco brief](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-resources-and-what-to-build)). The Inco rubric gave equal quarter weights to hidden mechanics, completeness, creativity and fun, and required the sponsor primitive to sit in the core loop, with a public prototype, demo video and open repository ([Inco brief](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-resources-and-what-to-build)). The organizer's own idea list opened with poker and games of chance, then fog-of-war strategy, guessing games such as Wordle and Hangman, and hidden-rarity lootboxes ([Inco brief](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-resources-and-what-to-build)). The placings ran against that list's order: **1st Mentalist (staked deduction), 2nd Zunno (Uno), 3rd Hextract (sealed-order fog of war)**, with the Hold'em entry pi River only an honorable mention alongside AEGYLAX and Glass Bridge Royale ([Inco winners](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners)). Zunno placed second although its builder describes client and server integration as still in progress ([Zunno README](https://github.com/ayush4345/zunno-inco)), which says the field was thin on finished games.

**Stellar Hacks: ZK Gaming.** The Stellar Development Foundation ran this on DoraHacks from 9 to 23 February with $10,000 in XLM split $5,000, $2,000, $1,250, $1,000 and $750; 99 builds came from 215 hackers ([DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/stellar-hacks-zk-gaming/winner)). No weighted rubric was published. The brief required a game prototype in which ZK is essential to a core mechanic (hidden information, fair resolution, provable outcomes, fog of war), stated that small games such as tic-tac-toe, Battleship or a simple card game were acceptable, and required testnet contracts wired to a shared game-hub contract, a working front end, open code and a two-to-three-minute video ([DoraHacks brief](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/stellar-hacks-zk-gaming/detail)). Placings: **1st Chickenz.io (real-time shooter with a proven outcome), 2nd xray.games (proven-score arcade), 3rd Stellar Poker, 4th Cosmic Coders, 5th Oppia zkArcade (Battleship and Wordle)** ([DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/stellar-hacks-zk-gaming/winner)). The two entries that most resemble classic hidden-information templates, poker and Battleship/Wordle, finished third and fifth. One search-engine summary misreported fifth place and was corrected against the official page.

**MLH x Midnight July hack.** A 48-hour event with 84 teams and seven unranked winners across privacy-infrastructure and gaming tracks ([Midnight](https://midnight.network/blog/celebrating-seven-winners-from-mlh-x-midnight-july-hack)). This is the only comparable with published reasoning, written by the organizer's developer-relations author and not by judges: Moonray won as the most complete build in the field and AmongUs Midnight because people wanted to play it ([Midnight](https://midnight.network/blog/celebrating-seven-winners-from-mlh-x-midnight-july-hack)).

**Inside Zama's own program.** Cipher21 won in Season 2 with blackjack; Zerk and SealPad won the same season with encrypted bets and sealed bids ([Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/announcing-the-developer-program-mainnet-season-2-winners)). Crackd, a Mastermind-style 1v1 code-breaking game on FHEVM with an AI "Vault" opponent, was submitted to the Season 3 Builder Track and does not appear on that season's winners list ([Zama forum](https://community.zama.org/t/crackd-a-confidential-1v1-code-breaking-game-where-your-code-is-encrypted-on-chain-fhevm/4506), [Season 3 winners](https://www.zama.org/post/announcing-the-developer-program-mainnet-season-3-winners)). Season 3 was not games-themed, so its absence there is weak evidence about game quality, but it is firm evidence of prior art.

Inference from these four cases: when hidden mechanics and creativity carry explicit weight, a new shape of secret outranks a familiar card port, and a finished, playable build outranks an ambitious partial one. The Zama criteria supplied by the user (gameplay and UX, confidentiality design, fairness, functionality, demo quality, code quality) contain no separate creativity line, which tilts the balance further toward completeness and a defensible confidentiality design than at Inco.

## Winners share one legible secret, a solo path and a visible proof

Seven patterns recur across the hidden-state winners. The first four are facts about what winners shipped; the reading of why they mattered is inference.

**One small secret per decision.** Mentalist needs one encrypted comparison per player per case; Hextract folds a whole turn into one ciphertext; Glass Bridge stores a whole map in one encrypted word ([Mentalist](https://github.com/adipundir/mentalist), [Hextract](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT), [Glass Bridge](https://github.com/gks2022004/glass-bridge-royale)). The counter-example is Bluff and Barrel, a four-player bluffing card game on Fhenix with no confirmed award, whose team reports about 1,200 FHE operations per game, about 7.9M gas for the deal, and a deal that had to be split into its own transaction after exceeding gas limits ([HackQuest](https://hackquest.io/en/projects/Bluff-and-Barrel)).

**Reveal timing is a designed rule.** Mentalist keeps the result locked until funds are final: "Until money can no longer move, the answer stays hidden" ([Mentalist README](https://github.com/adipundir/mentalist)). AmongUs Midnight reveals a role only on ejection, Hextract reveals an enemy only within two tiles, Moonray opens scores in a later window ([Midnight](https://midnight.network/blog/celebrating-seven-winners-from-mlh-x-midnight-july-hack), [Hextract](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT)).

**Coarse, lossy reveals create deduction.** Hextract's four proximity bands and AEGYLAX's noisy probes turn a single encrypted comparison into a clue the player must interpret ([Hextract](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT), [Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners)).

**A judge can play alone.** pi River has a bot, Hextract an offline AI trainer, Moonray and Mentalist are solo against a leaderboard or pool, Chickenz ships bots, BLACKOUT fills seats with on-chain bots that obey the same vision rules, and Crackd has an AI opponent ([pi River](https://github.com/thesithunyein/pi-river), [Hextract](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT), [Chickenz](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39887), [BLACKOUT](https://github.com/Anshv784/blackout)).

**Money or a leaderboard makes the secret matter.** Pari-mutuel pots (Mentalist), opener-weighted survivor pots (Glass Bridge), staked tables with a house fee (BLACKOUT) and lottery tie-ins (Zunno) recur ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners), [BLACKOUT](https://github.com/Anshv784/blackout)).

**Sessions are short.** Hextract caps at eight rounds, BLACKOUT at five minutes, Chickenz at three 30-second rounds, The Syndicate under two minutes ([Hextract](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT), [BLACKOUT](https://github.com/Anshv784/blackout), [Chickenz](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39887), [Colosseum](https://colosseum.com/arena/projects/explore/the-syndicate)).

**Solo builders with tight scope win often.** Moonray, Bank or Plank, Hunch, OneShot, VeritasX and CradleOS are each credited to one person ([Midnight](https://midnight.network/blog/celebrating-seven-winners-from-mlh-x-midnight-july-hack), [BitPinas](https://bitpinas.com/feature/ygg-play-verse8-buidlhack-results/), [Chainlink](https://chain.link/hack-26/projects/oneshot)).

### Demo and UX devices worth borrowing

The most transferable devices are the ones that let a judge confirm secrecy in seconds without reading code. BLACKOUT has a "Try to cheat" button that requests a live room's sealed accounts and shows the denial, plus a VERIFY tab that re-derives spawns and loot in the browser from the seed revealed at the end ([BLACKOUT](https://github.com/Anshv784/blackout)). Nightfall has an in-game X-ray panel that returns nothing for the world and for opponents' views ([Nightfall](https://github.com/codeswithroh/nightfall)). Daemon Hall keeps an arena sealed during play and then publishes the entire history for audit and replay ([Daemon Hall](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/daemon-hall-d14u4)).

Winners also wrote the judges' argument for them. Glass Bridge Royale's README carries a table explaining why plaintext, commit-reveal, ZK and a trusted server each fail for its mechanic ([Glass Bridge](https://github.com/gks2022004/glass-bridge-royale)); Hextract's README maps the entry to each rubric line ([Hextract](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT)); Stellar Poker shipped a slide deck explaining its cryptography simply ([Stellar Poker](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39840)). Candour about limits did not cost placings: Chickenz, first at Stellar, documents its remaining server trust, and the Stellar organizers praised the Real-World ZK winner Wraith for honesty about what was wired up ([Chickenz README](https://github.com/AshFrancis/chickenz), [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/stellar-hacks-zk/winner)). Verification is tightening: The Synthesis used AI judges that checked README claims against deployed code, and NEAR's pending hackathon pre-screens with agents that confirm each repo runs and matches its README ([Devfolio](https://devfolio.co/blog/synthesis/), [NEAR](https://nearhackathon.com/)).

Friction removal is the third family. Mentalist's investigation phase is wallet-free with a single transaction at commitment; Moonray, Glass Bridge and Hextract offer practice without a wallet; pi River uses Google sign-in with a silent play wallet and a house wallet that drips gas; Zunno relays moves through a forwarder so one signature replaces a popup per move ([Mentalist](https://github.com/adipundir/mentalist), [Moonray](https://github.com/kaleababayneh/moonray), [pi River](https://github.com/thesithunyein/pi-river), [Zunno](https://github.com/ayush4345/zunno-inco)). No winning team published measured end-to-end latency for an encrypted move, and no dedicated post-mortem from a 2026 winning game team was found.

## Six directions fit a 12-second chain and a 25-operation budget

The user's Sepolia measurements set the design envelope: a transaction confirms in about 12 s, an encrypted input costs about 7 s in the browser, reading your own hidden values takes 4–6 s (five values in one call), a public reveal takes 3–8 s, and about 25 chained encrypted operations fit in one transaction. Those numbers rule out anything real-time and rule in turn-based, simultaneous-commit and asynchronous designs, the same class the confidentiality-jam winners occupied. All round timings below are sums of the user's measurements; all operation counts are this report's design estimates and have not been tested.

Three mechanics from the winners fit poorly and are excluded. Real-time fog of war (BLACKOUT, Nightfall) runs at 20 ticks per second in an enclave and has no FHE path. Proof-of-play skill games (Chickenz, xray.games, Moonray) prove a private computation done on the device, which is a ZK job. Social deduction and party games (AmongUs Midnight, Herd) need several humans online, which collides with the requirement that a judge play alone with one wallet. Hidden-position prediction markets (Zerk, Epoch, VeilMarket) fit technically but are finance, are crowded, and Zerk has already won inside Zama's program.

What FHEVM adds that the winners lacked is specific. The enclave-based winners state their own limit: security rests on hardware (Mentalist) and the program cannot prove privacy by itself (BLACKOUT) ([Mentalist](https://github.com/adipundir/mentalist), [BLACKOUT](https://github.com/Anshv784/blackout)). Stellar Poker's builder argues multi-player shuffling cannot be done with ZK alone and so used an MPC committee ([Stellar Poker](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39840)). The commit-reveal entries each need a reveal step, and Glass Bridge's README notes the last revealer can abort ([Glass Bridge](https://github.com/gks2022004/glass-bridge-royale)). An FHEVM game can compute on the secret during play with no dealer committee, no reveal step and no hardware vendor, and its on-chain randomness can stay hidden after it is generated, which a VRF roll cannot do.

### 1. Bluffing-dealer three-card poker

Concept: a heads-up three-card hand against a contract dealer whose cards and bluff decision are encrypted, staked in confidential cUSDT. The inspiring winners are pi River for the bot-opponent and onboarding pattern, Stellar Poker for teachability, Cipher21 as proof that Zama rewards a house card game, and Glass Bridge Royale for the idea of selling one address a private peek ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners), [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/buidl/39840), [Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/announcing-the-developer-program-mainnet-season-2-winners), [Glass Bridge](https://github.com/gks2022004/glass-bridge-royale)). Encrypted: both hands, the dealer's decision to bluff, the ranking comparison, and every folded card permanently. If public, the dealer's hand is readable and a bluff cannot exist; with a server dealer, the house can see or alter cards. A hand fits the envelope as deal transaction (12 s), one batched read of three own cards (4–6 s), a plaintext call or fold, a showdown transaction (12 s) and a public reveal (3–8 s), roughly 31–38 s of waiting, and the user's tests already show the deal and blind ranking working in two transactions. The solo path is native. The main risk is perception: poker is the sponsor-template genre, hidden-hand card games placed on several different privacy stacks this year, and at Inco the Hold'em entry earned only an honorable mention. Crowding is low inside the Season 5 field (no poker found) and high everywhere else. The differentiators the evidence supports are the ones no confirmed 2026 poker winner documents: ranking on ciphertext without revealing the loser's cards, and a tested guarantee that mucked cards are never decryptable by anyone.

### 2. Blind crate auction with private clues

Concept: each round a crate with an encrypted random value goes to sealed-bid auction; every bidder holds a different private, noisy clue about its worth, and contract-run bots bid by the same rules. The inspiring winners are AEGYLAX for purchasable noisy hints followed by one sealed commitment ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners)), the sealed-bid finance winners Crafts, SealPad, NightPool and MaskBid for the argument judges already accept ([Arcium](https://arcium.substack.com/p/winners-of-solana-privacy-hack), [Zama](https://www.zama.org/post/announcing-the-developer-program-mainnet-season-2-winners), [MaskBid](https://chain.link/hack-26/projects/maskbid-the-dark-auction)), the non-winning ETHGlobal entry Fan Fare for second-price rules ([Fan Fare](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/fan-fare-jdjsq)), and BLACKOUT for bots bound by the same visibility rules ([BLACKOUT](https://github.com/Anshv784/blackout)). Encrypted: crate value, every clue, every bid including the bots', and losing bids forever. If public, the value is readable and there is nothing to estimate, and visible bids let the last bidder snipe. A crate runs as an opening transaction that draws the value and derives clues (12 s), a batched read of the player's clues (4–6 s), one encrypted bid (7 s), a closing transaction that compares bids and settles in cUSDT (12 s) and a public reveal of winner, price and true value (3–8 s): about 38–45 s. With three bots the estimate is 10–15 operations in each transaction. The main risk is that bots feel like a random-number generator and that an "auction" reads as DeFi to judges who have already rewarded SealPad; the game has to be about reading clues and the winner's curse, not about clearing a market. Crowding is the lowest of any direction: Inco listed blind auctions for in-game items as a possible genre and no winner anywhere built one ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/confidential-onchain-games)).

### 3. Sealed case: staked deduction with rationed encrypted questions

Concept: the contract seals a randomly generated solution (for example suspect, room and item); the player spends a small budget of yes-or-no questions answered privately on ciphertext, then stakes on one accusation. The inspiring winners are Mentalist for the staked single pick and the wallet-free thinking phase ([Mentalist](https://github.com/adipundir/mentalist)), The Last Alibi (ETHGlobal Lisbon, no prize) for a committed hidden case narrowed by a limited number of certified queries ([The Last Alibi](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/the-last-alibi-dctaw)), AEGYLAX for paid hints, and Crackd for the encrypted-feedback technique on FHEVM ([Zama forum](https://community.zama.org/t/crackd-a-confidential-1v1-code-breaking-game-where-your-code-is-encrypted-on-chain-fhevm/4506)). Encrypted: the solution, each answer (readable only by the asker), and the accusation until settlement. If public, the solution is read from storage; under commit-reveal, a hash over a handful of options is brute-forced, which is Mentalist's own argument. Each question costs an encrypted input, a transaction and a private read, about 23–25 s, and one to three comparisons; an accusation is about five operations plus payout. Four questions and an accusation make a case of roughly two minutes. Generating the answer from on-chain encrypted randomness removes the two weaknesses Mentalist's builder admits, a hand-written single-use case list and an author who must be trusted. The main risk is that it collapses into a guessing game, a neighbourhood that already holds number guessing and trivia in the Season 5 field, Zama's Wordle example and Crackd.

### 4. Hidden-lineup raid

Concept: split a fixed force across three lanes in one sealed submission and attack a standing encrypted defence; win two lanes to take the stake, then leave your own allocation behind as the next defence. Inspirations are One Arena's secret shot-and-save mode ([Genfinity](https://genfinity.io/2026/10/15/rosentica-natsuki-one-arena-solana-rwa-collectibles-application-layer-interview/)), Knock Order's secretly sequenced cards among the Dojo entrants ([Dojo GJ8](https://github.com/dojoengine/game-jams/blob/main/gj8/knock-order.md)) and The Syndicate's asynchronous PvP ([Colosseum](https://colosseum.com/arena/projects/explore/the-syndicate)). Encrypted: every allocation, attacker and defender, with only lane outcomes revealed. If public, the attacker reads the defence and picks a perfect counter. A raid is one encrypted input, one transaction and one public reveal, about 22–27 s, and about 10–12 operations for three comparisons, a budget check and a majority test. The solo path is asynchronous: the judge attacks defences seeded by the contract or left by earlier players. The main risk is depth; without a scouting layer of coarse reveals it reads as rock-paper-scissors with numbers, and sealed-move duels are already in the field. No confidential-jam winner of 2026 is an auto-battler or lineup game, which is an open lane by inference only.

### 5. Vault crossing with bought private peeks

Concept: the earlier hidden-minefield cash-out, rebuilt around Glass Bridge Royale's three disclosure modes: a layout sealed as one encrypted value, each step publicly revealing one cell, and a paid peek that shows one future cell to one address ([Glass Bridge](https://github.com/gks2022004/glass-bridge-royale)). Hunch and BlitzMine show the solo push-your-luck loop wins small sprints, and Bank or Plank shows it wins a pure game track ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/magicblock-solana-blitz-v6-41-mobile-apps-three-winners-one-weekend), [BlockchainGamer](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/news/42097/bank-or-plank-wins-verse8-ygg-plays-buidlhack-2026-hackathon/)). Encrypted: the whole layout from the first transaction. If rolled per step, a VRF outcome could be re-rolled or front-run; if stored in plaintext, it is read. A step is a transaction plus a public reveal, about 15–20 s, at two to four operations. The solo path is native and this is the cheapest direction to build. The main risk is crowding: the Season 5 field already contains a hidden-trap vault raid, a shell game and a mystery box, so this entry would be compared directly with close neighbours.

### 6. Sealed-orders micro-duel

Concept: a cut-down Hextract, both sides sealing one or two orders per round on a very small board, resolved on ciphertext, against a bot whose orders come from encrypted randomness. Hextract placed third at Inco and is the most FHE-shaped loop of the year: its contract never branches on secret state ([Hextract](https://github.com/Akhil-2310/HEXTRACT)). Infinite Gambit and the Dojo entrant Cipher show the same idea under commit-reveal, where every combat waits for the defender to return and reveal ([Infinite Gambit](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/infinite-gambit-856ps), [Cipher](https://github.com/dojoengine/game-jams/blob/main/gj8/cipher.md)). Encrypted: positions, orders and scan results. If public, the second mover always wins. A round is about 23–25 s in one transaction. The main risk is the operation budget: movement, scanning and combat on even a tiny board plausibly exceed 25 operations per round, forcing two transactions (about 37 s) or a rule set so thin it loses the appeal, and four weeks remain before the 14 December deadline. It is the best showcase and the worst schedule fit.

## The evidence keeps A in front, lifts B and forces C to change shape

**Candidate A, three-card poker against a bluffing hidden-hand dealer: still the lead, no longer novel.** The evidence cuts both ways. Against it: hidden-hand card games are the most crowded confidential genre of 2026, sponsors hand out poker templates, and at the closest comparable the Hold'em entry got an honorable mention while a deduction game won ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners)). For it: no poker entry exists in the Season 5 field as surveyed by the user; no 2026 hackathon-winning poker on Zama FHEVM, Fhenix, Arcium MPC or ZK mental poker was found; the confirmed poker winners rely on an enclave or an MPC committee; and none documents hand ranking on ciphertext or fold handling ([Inco docs](https://docs.inco.org/games/confidential-deck), [pi River](https://github.com/thesithunyein/pi-river)). The user's build already ranks two three-card hands blind in two transactions, which is the part others avoided. A is also the only candidate that satisfies the solo-judge requirement with no bots to design, and Cipher21 shows Zama's panel has rewarded a player-versus-contract card game before. The inference is that A wins on functionality, fairness and confidentiality design if it is finished and explained, and loses ground only if it presents as "encrypted poker." The bluffing dealer is what makes secrecy part of play instead of a dealer-honesty guarantee, so it should be the headline, with the never-decryptable muck as the second claim.

**Candidate B, sealed-bid crate auction with private clues: rises to the most original option.** More than nine 2026 winners used sealed bids or hidden positions, nearly all of them as finance products, and no auction or bidding game framed as a game appears among the winners reviewed. AEGYLAX, an honorable mention, validates the exact two-step shape B needs, private noisy hints and then one sealed commitment into a pool ([Inco](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-winners)). The evidence adds two requirements B did not have before: bots that provably follow the same rules, because every winning multiplayer game shipped a solo path, and a framing that keeps it out of the SealPad category. Its fun is unproven; no comparable exists to say a solo common-value auction against bots holds attention. B is the higher-variance choice: more distinctive than A on any novelty reading, weaker on readiness.

**Candidate C, code-breaker race for a pot: weakest as stated.** A Mastermind-style code-breaker with encrypted feedback already exists on Zama's FHEVM (Crackd) and did not place in Season 3; Zama's example repository has Wordle; the Season 5 field has number guessing; Inco's brief lists Wordle and Hangman as starter ideas; and the Battleship-and-Wordle entry finished fifth of five at Stellar ([Zama forum](https://community.zama.org/t/crackd-a-confidential-1v1-code-breaking-game-where-your-code-is-encrypted-on-chain-fhevm/4506), [Inco brief](https://www.inco.org/blog/summer-game-jam-resources-and-what-to-build), [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/stellar-hacks-zk-gaming/winner)). A "race" also needs rivals, which conflicts with solo judging. What rescues the idea is the first-place evidence from Mentalist: a staked deduction against a sealed answer wins when the thinking is free, the commitment is a single transaction and the fiction is strong. C survives only as direction 3 above, which is effectively a new design.

**The earlier minefield cash-out: proven loop, crowded shelf.** Glass Bridge Royale, Hunch, BlitzMine and Bank or Plank show the loop places and wins, and it is the cheapest to build. Inside the Season 5 field it has the closest neighbours of any idea. It is worth building only with the private-peek twist, or as a second mode inside another entry.

Whichever direction is chosen, the transferable devices are the same and are cheap: an in-app control that tries to read the hidden state and shows the refusal, a post-round unseal that lets anyone verify the outcome, a README table of why plaintext, commit-reveal, ZK and an enclave each fail for this mechanic, an explicit list of who is granted decryption of what and when, a wallet-free practice mode, and honest notes on remaining trust assumptions.

## Conclusion

The year's results say that confidential-game judges have seen encrypted cards many times and have almost never seen a game compute on a secret during play without a dealer, an enclave or a reveal step. That reframes the Season 5 decision. The scarce thing is not a genre; it is a finished, solo-playable loop in which an on-chain comparison of two hidden values is the moment of play, and where the player can watch the chain refuse to leak. Candidate A already has that moment in the blind ranking of two hands, and the cheapest route to a strong entry is to make the dealer's bluff and the never-revealed muck the story, then borrow the winners' proof devices.

The more interesting implication is about B. Sealed bidding is the one mechanic that every 2026 judging panel has already accepted as needing privacy, and nobody has yet made it fun. If time allows only one build, A is the defensible choice with four weeks left; if a second mode or a later season is possible, the crate auction is where the evidence shows open ground. Either way, the NEAR and Colosseum results due this week are worth one more look before code freezes.
